* Intro

In the article [[./Time.org][Is time a dimension?]] I tried to describe an understanding that time is not
a dimension. And in that article I promised to describe opposite option. But first let's
clarify the arguments against time as a dimension.

We looked at the situation with a car running over a cat. To survive, at least one
coordinate of the cat had to mismatch with the corresponding coordinate of the car. And if
time were a coordinate, then all the cat's spatial coordinates could coincide, except for
one - the time coordinate, and this would help the cat to escape. And such speculation
exists: it is claimed that the cat's coordinates could have been the same as those of a
car passing by the cat's house, but the cat was here, at this same point in space,
yesterday! That is, the time coordinates didn't match, and the cat is alive. This line of
reasoning can be refuted as follows:

just ask, *where is the cat now when a car passes this place on the road where it was
yesterday?* Indeed, if it was here yesterday, then today it's somewhere else, for
example, sitting next to the road and looking at a car. And it's alive: because at least
one of its coordinates - depth - doesn't match the car's. And yesterday it didn’t die not
because its time coordinate was different, but because yesterday its spatial coordinates
again didn’t coincide with the spatial coordinates of the car.

So, that was yesterday. And today the cat is somewhere again, sitting next to the road,
and *what makes you think its in a different time coordinate than the car's?!*

Yesterday, when it was saved, being in a different place again, *was cat's time coordinate
different from the car's?* Why?! Just because the cat was walking in this place on this road,
and the car was parked on the side of the road, nearby?

Physics thinks differently, it had the same time coordinates (and from the point of view
of the theory of relativity, too).

* Time as a dimension

Whether time is "really" a dimension in the same ontological sense as space is still open
to interpretation. To identify a point of an axis we need a coordinate - to find it, it is
an "address" of a point. If somewhere something happened, to identify this /event/ we need
its "coordinates". They are ~(x, y, z, t)~. This forces us to interpret time as a
coordinate.

* The time is a strange dimension

Space dimensions allow you to move along some of them independently, without to change
your other coordinates, the time is different: changing your "x", you change always your
"t" coordinate, so phisicists came to [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_transformation][Lorenz transformations]] and to the concept of
[[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime][Spacetime]]. So, 3 space dimensions and 1 non-spatial dimension form this, "new" object -
"Spacetime continuum"...

* What if time is not 1 dimension?

The idea that time might have more than 1 dimension is a real idea in theoretical
physics - not established fact, but something that has appeared in several serious
frameworks. There are different ideas, theories of multidimensional "time" (I quoted it!),
in general, some of them talk about additional time-dimensions, treating it as multiple
yesterdays, tomorrows, and another theories just talk about non-spatial dimensions (they
can be not exact time or something else)...

Let's start from esoteric view of the multidimensional time ideas.

* Esoteric interpretation of multidimensional time

First theory is the theory of Russian esoteric [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._D._Ouspensky][Piotr Ouspensky]] (1878) - a Russian philosopher and
esotericist known for his expositions of the early work of the Greek-Armenian teacher of
esoteric doctrine George Gurdjieff. He devoted quite a lot of attention to this issue and
described in detail what he means by "multidimensial time". Next I will briefly describe
his ideas.

** Ouspensky's multidimensial time

Ouspensky looked at the time axis:

#+begin_example
                  now
past <---- -  - ---+--- -  - ----> future

      birth                 death
#+end_example

and he asked himself, what if we have another dimension of the time? Then it would look
as:

#+begin_example

     _ _ _ _|_ _ _ _
     _ _ _ _|_ _ _ _ 
past -------+--------> future
#+end_example

ok, but what are these parallel lines? He thought that if we look at a regular axis, we
see numbers on it. On a parallel axis, we see the same numbers - the numbers make up the
axis. This is a definition from mathematics. It's key moment, lets remember it!

And he then reasoned as follows: on this axis we have time - the events of our lives are
isomorphic to it. OK, then the parralel lines? They *repeat* the same numbers... the same
events in our life. How to call the 2nd time dimension (5th dimension in such esoteric
"spacetime")? He called it /Eternity/ and... "recurrence". Yes, it is about [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_return][eternal
return]]. Here, Ouspensky made some interesting conclusion: looking at REPEATED time axis
(forming the time plane) he said - it is naturally recurrent! Time axis look as naturally
recurrent structure, like "laid cord". Look how it can be "proven": 

#+begin_example
     _ _ _ _￪_ _ _ _              who is living here?!
     _ _ _ _|_ _ _ _              and here too...?
     _ _ _ _|_ _ _ _
past -------+--------> future     you are living here!
#+end_example

So, your timeline is "obvious" - you are experiencing, living it! But others? Who are
experiencing them?! Not John (you), but some Kemal? Why??? They are parallel and *NO
DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THEM*. I think, we can suggest that John (you) are living them too,
but... when if you are here and you and the one?! We can suppose that it happens
some...where... You have one consciousness. No your duplicates (it is also very deep
philosophical problem: identity, ability to have full "copies", highly likely it is
impossible). So, Ouspensky suggested that your consciousness is here, on your
timeline. Then, some day (when? he suggested after the death), you will be able to pass
throung another, parallel timeline. The vertical time dimension is called "Eternity"
because every now goes on forever vertically. And it is called "recurrence" because
parallel timelines REPEAT EACH OTHER and the uniqueness of consciousness ensures
"repeatability" (recurrence) - but, to be honest, it is not 100% necessary (if
consciousnesses can be cloned, copied, be isolated). 

So, the a human history looks as:

birth event1 event2 ... event3 event4 death, then
birth event1 event2 ... event3 event4 death, then
...again and again
#+end_example

Then Ouspensky thought, hm, OK, it looks like torture (he didn't know about the movie
"Groundhog Day", but he invented its script), what can help here? Yet another time
dimension! And now we have 3 time dimensions:

 +------+ |.
 |      | +

        |
        :
        :
        :
  possibilities
#+end_example

The new dimension gives totality of possibility - it forms parallel time planes, you can
make decision, to choose your destiny:

#+begin_example

        \

#+end_example

British writer [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G._Bennett][J.G. Bennett]] supported these ideas.

** Criticism of the Ouspensky's model

First,

why are events the same on parallel timelines? Why are not possibilities actualized
immediately in the parallel timelines? If one timeline is ~{event1, event2, event3, ...}~
then another one could be ~{event5, event6, event7, ...}~.

Ouspensky answered it: *"The 5th dimension is repetition of the actualized."* But 5
dimensions alone do not mathematically imply recurrence. So, this position is accepted as
is - this follows from the definition of the 5th dimension introduced by him. He did it
using this his definition:

#+begin_quote
5th dimension - eternity - is the infinite existence of every moment of time.
#+end_quote

The second,

if he used this logic, then it can be used with vertical planes too, with the 6th
dimension (3rd time dimension), why not? Then we could have all 3 time dimensions as
repeating of one timeline - it is *underdetermination problem*: the geometry doesn't
uniquely determine which temporal coordinate represents recurrence and which represents
possibility, they both can represent reccurence!

There is an even deeper *symmetry problem*: suppose we rotate this 6-dimensional
coordinate system. In ordinary geometry, there's nothing intrinsically special about ~0x~
versus ~0y~, *if the geometry is /isotropic/, we can rotate our axes*.

So, I could answer:

- it is so by the definition of Ouspensky
- his system is /anisotropic/ by definition

His vision is:

- 3D = space
- 4D = time
- 5D = eternity or recurrence
- 6D = possibility

and then the "period" is complete (he said it explicitly). *Ouspensky made a specific
metaphysical choice about what the additional dimensions mean.*

** Antiquity

Pythagoras believed in such possibility. Eudemus of Rhodes repeated it, the Stoics also
tought that "...the universe is periodically destroyed in an immense conflagration
(ekpyrosis), and then experiences a rebirth (palingenesis). These cycles continue for

eternity again, but no wording about other dimensions of times. Does "eternity" imply the
existence of an additional dimension of time?

The answer is that many schools and philosophers thought about time and saw it as
different structures but maybe only Ouspensky did it in so geometric manner allowed us to
talk about time dimensions directly, explicitly. All others (Hindu cosmology, Yoga
Vedanta, Buddhism, Greek philosophy, Neoplatonism, Hermetic traditions, Gnosticism,
Kabbalah even, Nietzsche...) did not talk about DIMENSIONS of TIME. They used other
terminology, not geometric one.

Absolutely. The idea that time might have more than one
dimension is a real idea in theoretical physics—not established fact, but something that
has appeared in several serious frameworks.

The key distinction is:



And it gets very weird.

1. What does “another time dimension” actually mean?

Imagine ordinary space.

You can move:

left ↔ right
forward ↔ backward
up ↔ down

Those are three independent spatial directions.

In ordinary relativity, there is one independent temporal direction:

earlier ↔ later

So spacetime has coordinates

(t,x,y,z)

or, more formally, a metric with one time-like direction and three space-like directions.

A universe with two time dimensions might instead have coordinates such as

(t1,t2,x,y,z).

The important part is that t1 and t2 would need to be genuinely independent directions—not simply two ways of measuring the same time.

That's a much bigger change than saying “there are two clocks.”

2. Why don't we normally see a second time?

This is where things become fascinating.

In relativity, spacetime has a particular mathematical structure called a metric signature.

Our familiar spacetime can roughly be represented as

(−,+,+,+)

where the minus sign corresponds to the time-like direction.

A two-time-dimensional spacetime could have something like

(−,−,+,+,+).

That seemingly tiny change causes enormous problems.

For example, consider a particle.

In ordinary relativity, its energy-momentum relation looks schematically like

E2=p2c2+m2c4.

With additional time-like dimensions, the corresponding relation can acquire additional negative-sign terms.

This can produce things like:

negative-norm states
instabilities
problems defining probabilities
causality problems
particles with unusual energy behavior
difficulty defining a sensible initial-value problem

In other words, nature seems to strongly prefer one time direction.

3. One of the biggest problems: causality

This is probably the most intuitive problem.

Suppose you have one time dimension:

past -----------------> future


You can ask:

What happened before this event?

And:

What can happen after this event?

That's relatively clean.

But with two time dimensions:

             t₂
              ↑
              |
              |
              |
              +--------→ t₁


There isn't necessarily one unique direction called “the future.”

You could move through the t1 direction.

Or through t2.

Or some combination of both.

That can destroy the simple causal ordering we're accustomed to.

4. The “two times = time travel” idea

This is where popular discussions sometimes get misleading.

People sometimes hear:

“If there are two time dimensions, you could move sideways through time.”

That's an interesting visualization, but it doesn't automatically mean you could travel into your own past.

A second time coordinate is not necessarily a time machine.

It's more like adding another independent axis to the geometry of spacetime.

Compare:

One spatial dimension
←---------→

Two spatial dimensions
       ↑
       |
       |
←------+------→
       |
       |


Now imagine doing something analogous with time:

             t₂
              ↑
              |
              |
              +--------→ t₁


There would be many possible directions through temporal space.

But the physical interpretation of those directions is extremely nontrivial.

5. A serious idea: Itzhak Bars and “two-time physics”

One of the best-known modern approaches is associated with physicist Itzhak Bars.

Bars developed a framework called 2T physics (“two-time physics”).

The basic idea is genuinely interesting:

Maybe a theory with two time dimensions can exist at a deeper level, while the universe we experience effectively has only one observable time dimension.

So rather than saying:

“We obviously live in a universe with two times.”

the idea is closer to:

“Perhaps the fundamental theory has two times, but our ordinary 3+1-dimensional physics is a particular manifestation of it.”

That's a much more sophisticated proposition.

6. The really strange part: one-time physics could emerge from two-time physics

Imagine a higher-dimensional theory with:

4+2

dimensions:

4 spatial dimensions
2 temporal dimensions.

But because of special constraints/symmetries, an observer could effectively experience:

3+1.

This is somewhat analogous to how a 3D object could cast a 2D shadow.

The lower-dimensional description can contain the physics you actually observe without exposing every feature of the underlying structure.

In 2T physics, different “gauge choices” can produce different-looking one-time physical systems from the same underlying theory.

That's one of the most interesting motivations for the idea.

7. An analogy

Imagine a person living on a sheet of paper.

They experience:

x → 
y ↑


Their entire universe appears 2D.

But suppose there's a 3D object above the paper.

The person might never directly perceive the third spatial dimension.

Different intersections of the object with their plane could appear to them as completely different 2D objects.

Likewise, in some higher-dimensional theories, our familiar universe could be one particular “slice” or description of a deeper structure.

The analogy isn't mathematically exact, but it's useful.

8. Another important idea: multiple times in string theory

Extra dimensions appear naturally in string theory and related frameworks.

Usually, however, the additional dimensions are spatial, not temporal.

For example, string theory can require more than four spacetime dimensions.

But adding extra time-like dimensions is much more troublesome than adding spatial ones.

This distinction matters enormously.

A theory with:

3+7

could mean 3 spatial dimensions and 7 additional spatial dimensions.

That's very different from:

8+2

with two time dimensions.

The latter creates serious consistency issues.

9. Why extra time dimensions are dangerous mathematically

Here's a deeper reason.

Quantum mechanics requires probabilities to behave properly.

Very roughly, quantum states have an inner product that lets us calculate probabilities:

P∼∣ψ∣2.

The mathematical structure needs to behave nicely so that probabilities remain:

P≥0

and total probability can be normalized.

Multiple time directions can introduce states with problematic signs—sometimes called ghost states.

A ghost state isn't a little ghost 👻.

It's a state whose mathematical properties can cause the theory to have negative probabilities or catastrophic instabilities.

That's obviously a problem if you're trying to construct a physical universe.

10. There's also a problem with predicting the future

This one is particularly deep.

Ordinary physics often works as an initial-value problem.

You specify the state of a system at one moment:

state at t0

and the equations determine what happens later.

For example:

d2xdt2=F(x,t).

Give me the appropriate initial conditions, and I can evolve the system forward.

With two time dimensions,

∂2ϕ∂t12+∂2ϕ∂t22+⋯

the question becomes:

What exactly counts as “initial data”?

A single moment isn't necessarily enough.

And mathematical equations involving multiple time-like directions can fail to have the well-behaved evolution that ordinary physics relies on.

This is one reason theoretical physicists are extremely cautious about multiple-time theories.

11. Could consciousness experience two times?

This gets philosophical very quickly.

Suppose there were two fundamental temporal coordinates:

(t1,t2).

Would humans perceive both?

Not necessarily.

Our brains themselves operate as physical systems embedded in whatever effective spacetime exists.

If the laws governing matter restrict macroscopic processes to a particular temporal direction, then our experience could still be:

past → present → future

even if the deeper theory contained another time-like dimension.

So:

fundamental dimensionality ≠ directly perceived dimensionality.

That's a very important distinction.

12. Could the second time dimension be hidden?

Yes. This is one of the main ways such ideas are made plausible.

Extra dimensions can potentially be:

Compactified

An additional dimension could be curled up at an incredibly tiny scale.

Imagine a garden hose.

From far away:

------------------


It looks like a 1D line.

Up close, however, you discover a circular direction around the hose.

So an apparently one-dimensional object actually has another dimension.

Something similar happens in some higher-dimensional theories.

However, hiding a time dimension is much harder than hiding a spatial dimension because of the causality and stability issues mentioned above.

13. Could time have a direction because of entropy?

Now we get into a related but different idea.

The arrow of time is often associated with entropy.

The fundamental microscopic laws are frequently approximately time-reversible.

But macroscopic processes seem to have a preferred direction:

low entropy → high entropy


For example:

egg intact → egg broken


rather than spontaneously:

egg broken → egg intact


This raises an important question:

If the fundamental universe has no inherent “past → future” direction, where does our experience of time's direction come from?

That's the arrow of time problem.

But note:

the arrow of time is NOT evidence for multiple time dimensions.

They're separate ideas.

14. What about the idea that the future already exists?

That's another fascinating concept connected to time, but again, it's different.

In the block universe interpretation of relativity, spacetime can be thought of as a four-dimensional structure containing events at different times.

Instead of reality being:

past → present → future


you might imagine:

        spacetime
┌─────────────────────────┐
│ past │ present │ future │
└─────────────────────────┘


All events are part of the spacetime geometry.

This doesn't mean the future is necessarily “predetermined” in the philosophical sense; different interpretations of physics treat that question differently.

But it gives us a radically different picture of what time is.

15. Now combine that with a second time

Here's where things become really strange.

Ordinary block universe:

(x,y,z,t)

Think of a 4D spacetime block.

Two-time universe:

(x,y,z,t1,t2)

Now reality could resemble a 5D geometric structure with two temporal directions.

Instead of one timeline:

past ───── present ───── future


you could conceptually have a temporal plane:

                t₂
                 ↑
                 |
                 |
                 |
                 +──────────→ t₁


Our experienced history could correspond to a particular trajectory through that temporal plane.

That's one reason the concept is so philosophically mind-bending.

16. Could there be different histories?

Potentially, depending on the theory.

Imagine:

                 History B
                    /
                   /
History A --------●-------- History C
                   \
                    \
                 History D


You might be tempted to interpret those as alternate universes or timelines.

But a second time dimension does not automatically produce parallel universes.

Those are separate concepts.

You need additional assumptions about the physics to get branching histories, many worlds, etc.

17. There are actually several different “multiple time” ideas

This is important because people often lump them together.

A. Two-time physics

Fundamental spacetime has more than one time-like dimension.

Example: Bars' 2T physics.

B. Extra dimensions where time-like directions appear

Some higher-dimensional mathematical theories can have multiple time-like directions.

This doesn't necessarily mean they describe our universe.

C. Emergent time

Time might not be fundamental at all.

It could emerge from a deeper quantum structure.

D. Multiple arrows of time

Different physical processes could potentially define different arrows of time.

That's not the same as multiple dimensions.

E. Branching timelines

Many possible histories could exist.

Again, not automatically multiple time dimensions.

F. Time as a spatial dimension

The block-universe picture treats time geometrically alongside space, but that does not make time literally another spatial dimension.

18. Could there be 2D time but only one “experienced” time?

This is probably the most interesting possibility.

Suppose fundamental reality has:

(t1,t2)

but some physical constraint forces ordinary matter to evolve along something like

t2=f(t1).

Then an observer experiences only the resulting one-dimensional trajectory.

Visually:

          t₂
           ↑
           |
        •  |
      •    |
    •      |
  •        |
  +--------------→ t₁


The underlying temporal space has two dimensions, but your physical history follows one particular path.

That's conceptually similar to how an ant walking on a 2D surface experiences a 1D path.

19. Could we move through the second time?

This is where physics currently gives us no established answer.

If a second time dimension actually existed and were physically accessible, you'd need to understand:

what determines motion in t1?
what determines motion in t2?
what is causality?
what determines the observer's proper time?
how does quantum mechanics work?
how do particles propagate?
how is energy defined?
why don't we observe violations of causality?
why do macroscopic systems appear to have one time direction?

A theory has to answer all of those.

Simply adding another t to an equation isn't enough.

20. Does general relativity allow it?

Mathematically, general relativity can be formulated on manifolds with different metric signatures.

But our observed universe has one time-like direction.

General relativity itself doesn't give us evidence that there is a second physically accessible time dimension.

And simply changing the signature from something like

(−,+,+,+)

to

(−,−,+,+,+)

doesn't give you a viable universe automatically.

You need a consistent physical theory.

21. Is there experimental evidence?

This is the critical bottom line:

No confirmed experimental evidence currently establishes a second time dimension.

Physics has extremely strong experimental support for spacetime with:

3 space+1 time.

Multiple-time theories are speculative theoretical possibilities.

That's not the same thing as saying they're nonsense.

There are many ideas in theoretical physics that are mathematically sophisticated and worth investigating without having experimental confirmation.

22. Why would physicists investigate it at all?

Because sometimes strange mathematical ideas reveal unexpected relationships.

For example, a higher-dimensional theory might potentially explain or unify things that look unrelated in ordinary physics.

The attraction of approaches like 2T physics is partly that apparently different one-time theories can emerge as different descriptions of a deeper theory.

That's potentially powerful.

It's similar in spirit to discovering that two seemingly different descriptions of nature are actually manifestations of one underlying structure.

23. The deepest philosophical question

The really profound question isn't necessarily:

“Can I travel through a second time?”

It's:

What is time fundamentally?

Physics doesn't have a universally accepted answer.

We know extraordinarily well how to model time.

But that's not quite the same as understanding what time is.

Is time:

fundamental?
emergent?
geometric?
quantum?
relational?
an ordering of events?
connected to entropy?
something that emerges from quantum entanglement?
one aspect of a larger structure?

Different approaches to fundamental physics give different answers.

24. The most mind-bending possibility

Here's a speculative picture—not established physics, but useful for thinking.

Imagine that what we call:

“the universe evolving in time”

is actually a lower-dimensional manifestation of a more complicated structure.

Perhaps fundamental reality looks more like:

(x1,x2,x3,t1,t2,…)

and the particular physical conditions of our universe select one effective temporal direction.

Then “the passage of time” could be analogous to the way a lower-dimensional observer experiences a trajectory through a higher-dimensional structure.

In that picture, time as we experience it wouldn't necessarily be fundamental.

It would be an emergent feature of the way our physical world sits inside a deeper geometry.

That's one of the reasons these ideas are so fascinating.

The important reality check

If you've encountered videos/posts saying something like:

“Scientists proved there are TWO dimensions of time”

be skeptical.

That's almost certainly exaggerating a theoretical result.

The scientifically accurate statement is closer to:

Physicists have explored mathematically consistent theories involving multiple time-like dimensions, including frameworks in which familiar one-time physics can emerge from a theory with two times. But there is currently no experimental evidence that our universe actually contains a second observable time dimension.

And that's already an incredibly strange possibility.

If you want to go really deep, the next step is to look at exactly how a 2-time universe works mathematically—the metric signature, light cones, causality, the 2T-physics constraints, and how ordinary 3+1 spacetime can emerge from a 4+2 theory. That's where the idea goes from philosophical to genuinely mind-bending physics.
